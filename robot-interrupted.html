Robot Interrupted: Interactive Street Art Installation & Chalk-Drawing Robot

Robot Interrupted is an interactive street art installation designed to encourage passersby to engage with a robot drawing chalk art on the street. Debuting at the Loop Arts Fest, a one-day festival organized by the Chicago Loop Art Alliance on October 17, the installation features a touch screen interface for users to participate directly in the artmaking process. Attendees are encouraged to draw along with the robot, reminding people that art is a collective process and takes many forms by blending UX design, robotics, and visual design to build civic community engagement in an open public setting.

1. The Vision: Bringing Robotics to a Public Setting

Robotics research is frequently confined to academic laboratories, industrial manufacturing floors, or closed corporate demonstrations. For everyday citizens, encounters with autonomous machines are almost exclusively utilitarian—such as automated delivery rovers navigating sidewalks or commercial vacuum cleaners rolling across office floors.

Robot Interrupted fundamentally inverts this relationship. Designed as an interactive street art installation, the project brings robotics into the public realm to demystify emerging technology through playful, spontaneous co-creation. The robot will be part of the Loop Arts Fest, a vibrant one-day festival organized by the Chicago Loop Art Alliance on October 17.

"Robot Interrupted is an interactive street art installation designed to encourage passersby to engage with a robot drawing chalk art on the street. Attendees are encouraged to draw along with the robot, reminding people that art is a collective process and takes many forms."
— Project Mission · Loop Arts Fest 2026

We chose chalk on city pavement as our physical medium deliberately. Chalk is tactile, ephemeral, nostalgic, and universally approachable. It eliminates the intimidation of making permanent errors and transforms public sidewalks into an open, democratic canvas where anyone—regardless of age, background, or artistic training—can participate.

3. Interface Design: Frictionless Touch Screen Interaction

In a bustling street festival setting like the Loop Arts Fest, passersby will not read tutorials or sign up for accounts. If an interaction requires more than five to ten seconds of orientation, potential participants simply walk away. Interface designer Helly Patel architected an intuitive, high-visibility touch screen interface tailored specifically for rapid public onboarding.

Key interface affordances include:

  • Direct Touchscreen Canvas: A fluid drawing surface where participants can immediately sketch organic strokes, geometric loops, or letters with their fingers or an accessible stylus.
  • Generative Prompt Modifiers: For passersby who feel self-conscious about drawing on a blank screen, the interface provides playful generative seeds—such as floral tessellations, rhythmic waveforms, and algorithmic spirals—that attendees can trigger and modify.
  • Real-Time Spatial Preview: The touch interface displays a live overhead map reflecting the robot's physical coordinates, chalk reservoir status, and heading on the pavement, establishing immediate visual correspondence between screen input and physical motion.
  • Inclusive Visual Tokens: Designed with high-contrast UI components compliant with WCAG AAA standards, large touch targets suitable for all hand sizes and motor abilities, and universal iconography that bypasses language barriers.

4. Usability Testing & Interaction Design in the Wild

My primary focus as interaction designer and usability researcher is bridging the behavioral divide between curious passersby and an active mobile robot. Unlike a controlled lab study, conducting usability testing in the wild requires accounting for unpredictable pedestrian traffic, changing ambient lighting, background noise, and diverse social dynamics.

Our usability testing and interaction design methodology revolves around three core pillars:

  • Pedestrian Approach & Dwell Analysis: Evaluating how signage, visual cues on the touch screen, and the robot's mechanical mark-making draw pedestrian curiosity and convert fleeting foot traffic into active participants.
  • Turn-Taking & Co-Presence: Establishing clear communication protocols so users understand when the robot is listening to screen input, when it is translating strokes to pavement, and when it invites the participant to add their own physical chalk marks beside it.
  • Physical Ergonomics & Safety: Designing the physical kiosk setup so that wheelchair users, standing adults, and young children can comfortably reach the touch screen, while ensuring the mobile robot maintains a safe physical buffer zone with LiDAR proximity stops.

5. Robotics Design & Street Mark-Making

Led by Muzammil Shaikh, the robotics design addresses the complex physical challenge of translating digital strokes into continuous physical chalk marks on textured urban pavement:

  • Mobile Robotic Base (Turtlebot): Utilizes a compact, differential drive mobile robot engineered for precise rotational positioning and safe, controlled locomotion in human-shared public plazas.
  • Custom Servo-Driven Chalk Dispenser: A custom mechanical rig deploys chalk sticks using controlled downward spring tension. The mechanism can lift the chalk during transit to execute discontinuous lines without smudging previous drawings.
  • Kinematic Stroke Translation: Digital coordinate arrays captured from the touch screen are parsed by an event-driven Python backend, smoothed using the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm, and converted into linear velocity ($v$) and angular velocity ($\omega$) vectors.
  • Outdoor Surface Compensation: Street pavement possesses varying friction and micro-topography. Adaptive wheel-velocity control and odometry compensation ensure strokes maintain geometric fidelity across asphalt, pavers, and sidewalk concrete.

6. The "Interrupted" Dynamic: Art as a Collective Process

In conventional industrial automation and computer science, an "interruption" is treated as an exceptional error condition—a fault that stops execution. In Robot Interrupted, we intentionally redefine interruption as an essential gesture of community co-creation:

  • Drawing Along With the Robot: Attendees at the Loop Arts Fest are invited not only to touch the screen, but also to grab physical sidewalk chalk and draw right alongside the robot on the street.
  • Adaptive Path Redirection: When a participant draws a chalk line across the robot's intended path, sensory detectors recognize the physical mark and prompt the robot to either weave around it, incorporate it into its current drawing, or yield the turn.
  • Celebrating Collective Art: The resulting sidewalk mural is never the sole product of an algorithm or an individual human; it is a living, multi-layered tapestry of collective creativity that demonstrates how art can take diverse, spontaneous forms.

7. Loop Arts Fest Exhibition & Community Impact

The project blends UX design, robotics, and visual design to build civic engagement by bringing robotics directly to a public setting. Showcasing at the Loop Arts Fest on October 17 provides an invaluable real-world testbed to assess human-robot interaction in civic spaces, demonstrating that technology can foster human connection, play, and communal joy.
