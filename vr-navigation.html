VR Navigation Training: Haptic Sensory Feedback for Non-Visual Wayfinding

Visually impaired individuals face a dearth of tools to navigate indoor spaces safely. By pairing an interactive virtual reality simulation with Arduino-controlled haptic vibration motors, users safely build robust spatial mental models of unfamiliar architectural layouts without fear of physical harm.

My Role Developer and Researcher
The Team 1 Developer/Researcher, 1 Mentoring Professor
Project Type Research, Accessibility Design, Virtual Reality
Timeline Aug 2021 – May 2022
Tools Unity 3D (C#), Arduino Microcontrollers, Vibration Actuators

1. The Assistive Gap in Indoor Wayfinding

Outdoor navigation for blind and visually impaired people is supported by well-established aids: white canes, guide dogs, tactile paving, and audio-guided GPS systems. In stark contrast, indoor spaces present irregular, high-density obstacles: shifting furniture, recessed alcoves, stairs, and narrow doorways.

Learning an unfamiliar indoor floorplan in the physical world carries a constant risk of physical collision, bruising, and high cognitive stress. The goal of this research was to investigate whether virtual reality, decoupled from physical danger, could serve as an effective pre-visit cognitive mapping tool.

"Visually impaired people face a dearth of research around tools to navigate indoor settings, having tools better equipped for outdoor settings like guide dogs and canes. In an effort to design a tool better equipped for indoor settings, I designed a virtual reality experience in which the user would navigate their environment using vibration motors to develop their spatial memory without fear of physical harm."
— Project Thesis & Research Statement

2. Physical Computing & Tactile Encoding

Working under the guidance of my computer science faculty mentor at TCNJ, I engineered a low-latency bridge between a Unity 3D simulation and a wearable Arduino hardware array.

We designed a continuous tactile encoding scheme using miniature eccentric rotating mass (ERM) vibration motors:

  • Clearance State (> 2.0 meters): Complete motor silence, indicating uninhibited movement forward.
  • Approach Warning (0.8m – 2.0m): Low-frequency rhythmic pulsing that accelerates as proximity to an obstacle decreases.
  • Immediate Boundary (< 0.5 meters): High-frequency continuous buzzing, simulating the tactile boundary feedback of a cane contact.
  • Binaural Ambient Anchors: Spatialized sound cues (such as the steady hum of a ventilation fan or the click of a wall clock) provided directional reference points.

3. Evaluation & Spatial Memory Formation

Participants completed exploratory trials navigating virtual rooms with varying obstacle densities. Following virtual exploration, participants performed non-visual sketch-mapping and orientation recall tasks.

The findings confirmed that tactile haptic feedback in VR successfully stimulated spatial cognitive map construction:

  • Transferable Orientation: Users identified key room landmarks (doors, tables, exits) and retained their relative spatial orientations.
  • Zero Physical Hazard: Users could freely test navigational boundaries, bump into virtual walls, and self-correct with complete psychological safety.
  • Low Hardware Overhead: The prototype utilized accessible, off-the-shelf microcontrollers and motors that cost under $40, establishing a practical model for clinics, schools, and transit centers.

4. Impact & Research Summary
