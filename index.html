About

I am an Accessible Creative Technologist and Human-Computer Interaction researcher based in Chicago. Currently, I am a Graduate Research Assistant at PUSH Studio and an M.S. student in Human-Computer Interaction at DePaul University.

My research centers on developing interfaces for emerging technologies that prioritize human agency, dignity, and accessibility. At PUSH Studio, I work on non-invasive brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) that enable people with limb differences to participate in digital art therapy and operate assistive robotic tools using electroencephalogram (EEG) signals.

Before graduate study, I worked as a Systems Engineer at Bank of America, optimizing automated continuous deployment infrastructure. My undergraduate research at The College of New Jersey (TCNJ) focused on designing virtual reality wayfinding tools with Arduino haptic vibration actuators, helping blind and low-vision individuals build spatial cognitive maps of indoor spaces without physical risk. I also co-developed the musical puzzle platformer Mason & Strings, which was published on Steam and selected for the TooManyGames indie showcase.

Outside the lab, I am a two-time marathon runner working toward completing all six World Marathon Majors, and I am currently training for my first triathlon.

Undergraduate Research · TCNJ · 2021 – 2022

VR Navigation Training: Haptic Sensory Feedback for Non-Visual Wayfinding

Thendral Prabu (Lead Developer & Researcher), Faculty Advisor

Indoor environments present significant navigational barriers for visually impaired people, who frequently lack safe tools to explore unfamiliar layouts. Engineered an accessible virtual reality training environment paired with custom Arduino hardware driving eccentric rotating mass (ERM) vibration motors. Users explore virtual architectural floorplans via tactile distance feedback and spatialized audio, safely building durable cognitive maps prior to visiting physical spaces.
