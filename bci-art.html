Brain-Computer Interface and Gesture Control for Robotic Drawing & Robotic Control

A year of research into whether a person without fine motor control could direct a drawing robot. After the EEG headset proved too unreliable, the project moved to camera-based gesture tracking. This is an early-stage feasibility study.

My Role Graduate Research Assistant
Laboratory PUSH Studio · DePaul University CDM
Project Type BCI Research, Assistive Technology, Participatory Design
Timeline 2025 – Present
Technologies Emotiv Insight EEG, Python, MediaPipe, DexArm

1. Context & Purpose

Creative expression through visual art has well-documented benefits. People who have experienced amputations, spinal cord injuries, or limb differences often can’t hold a brush, stylus, or mouse in the usual way. This project asks what drawing could look like if the input came from something other than the hands.

The project is inspired by Münßinger et al. and McClinton et al., who built BCI systems for making art in VR.

2. Where We Started

For the first year we worked with the Emotiv Insight, a consumer EEG headset. We read prior work on BCI and robot-assisted art, tested possible commands, and built a Bezier curve tool so the Dexarm could draw smooth lines. An early, formative interview with an art therapist shaped how we approached the work. We demonstrated left and right control at DePaul’s Jarvis Innovation Showcase in May 2026. We then added push and pull, and found the system could not reliably recognize four directions.

3. Where We Are Now

After training the Emotiv Insight on additional directions, we found it could not reliably recognize four. Data from partner labs told us that the Emotiv Epoc, the higher end hardware sold by Emotiv, is similarly unreliable, and following this we have begun training MediaPipe models to pair gestural motion with the robot arm. We are now training gesture models that pair hand and face motion with the robot arm, using MediaPipe landmark tracking as the base. The open questions are whether gestures can map to consistent drawing directions and which ones are comfortable to hold for long.
